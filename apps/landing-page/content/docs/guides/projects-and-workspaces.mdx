title: Projects and workspaces
description: How organizations, offices, and projects fit together, plus your personal workspace and member roles.
---

TurboPlan organizes everything into a three-level hierarchy. Understanding it
makes it clear where your projects live and who can see them.

## The hierarchy

- **Organization** — the top-level container, such as a government agency.
- **Office** — a division within an organization.
- **Project** — the unit of work, living inside an office.

## Your personal workspace

Every user gets an automatically created personal workspace: your own
organization and office. This is where new projects always start — they are
created there as drafts, never directly inside a government organization.

When a project is ready, it can be submitted to a government organization for
review. That review destination is separate from the personal workspace where
the project was created.

## Switching between workspaces

The sidebar includes an organization and office switcher. Use it to move
between your personal workspace and any other organizations or offices you
belong to. Outside of a project, the sidebar shows your list of organizations;
inside a project, it shows the project's fixed feature modules.

## Members and roles

Membership applies at each level of the hierarchy, and each member has a role:

- **Owner** — full control, including managing members.
- **Editor** — can make changes to the project's content.
- **Viewer** — read-only access.

Roles inherit downward: a role granted at the organization level carries into
its offices and projects. You manage who has access — and at what role — from
the **Members** view of a project, office, or organization.

## Timeline

Each project has a **Timeline** — a read-only activity log of changes across the
project over time. It records what happened and when, so you can see a project's
history at a glance. There's nothing to act on here beyond viewing; it's a
reference for tracking how a project evolved.
